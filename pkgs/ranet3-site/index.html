Why ranet3Protocols and routing

Why ranet3

Standard protocols

Peers only need standard IKEv2, ESP, Babel and SRv6.

Interoperability is checked against:

strongSwan
IKEv2 and ESP sessions with Ed25519 keys
BIRD
Babel routes with RTT metrics, matched route for route
Linux
Kernel seg6local acts on the SRv6 headers ranet3 writes

Any other router or switch that supports IKEv2 with Ed25519 keys, Babel and SRv6 should interoperate as well.

Common mesh VPN and SD-WAN tools (Tailscale, NetBird, ZeroTier, Nebula, etc.) admit only nodes that run their own agent, and ranet3 runs beside them on the same host.

Latency routing

Babel measures the round trip of every link and routes through a relay whenever the direct link is slower.

RTT is measured continuously, and packet loss and outages count against a link. Traffic always takes the fastest path, moving back to the direct link once it is faster again.

Segment routing

A policy on source and destination prefix sends traffic through chosen waypoints and out at a chosen exit. Segment routing is implemented in userspace and works even on macOS, whose XNU kernel has no support for it.

Waypoints can come in any order the user specifies and may repeat, e.g. A->B->C->B->D with D as the exit.
